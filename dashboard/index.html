How the value is fitted

The scatter is the fit itself: each dot is one of the 128 tokenized protocols in the snapshot, log market cap against log TVL of the token’s whole family — the snapshot splits one token’s TVL across sibling deployments, so the fit pairs a token’s market cap with its family’s total TVL, not one deployment’s. The line is the fitted power law. 17 dots are grey: protocols more than 3 log-points from the line — their market cap per family TVL is twenty times the fitted value or a twentieth of it — so the fit runs on the other 111 amber dots, and the slope barely moves (0.55 to 0.57), which is the check the power law is not an artifact of the extremes. The fit is real but noisy (R² 0.43): it ranks protocols, it does not predict a price. Launch odds are the snapshot’s own tokenization rate by age among protocols like the ones on this board, shrunk toward the 50% average: 31% of protocols under six months have a token, 40% at six months to two years, 60% at two to three years, 79% past three years — times a category factor for what age alone cannot explain (Risk Curators 0.60: only 5 of 39 have a token; Lending 1.19; Basis Trading 1.19). Momentum and age-as-value were tested as fitted factors and both failed — momentum’s coefficient is unstable across split halves, age adds nothing to value — so momentum only trims the TVL input and age enters only through launch odds. Category multipliers are shrunk deviations of value per TVL, capped at 0.7 to 1.4; a category with no fitted member gets 1.000. Board rows are valued on their own TVL, so a protocol that is one deployment of a larger family is valued conservatively.

Source: the sealed snapshot of 758 protocols, as of . “Implied value” is a model of what a token launch has historically been worth for a protocol of this size and kind. It is not a price, a promise, or a prediction that a token will launch. Farming carries risk: only capital you can lose, on the protocol’s own app, and never pay anyone to claim for you.
